# Delaware Division of Corporations — How to Form a New Business Entity

**Citation:** Delaware Division of Corporations. "How to Form a New Business Entity."
corp.delaware.gov.
**Analytical level:** Structural (very weak fit — see note below)
**Most relevant design decisions:** Q1

**Note on fit:** this is an administrative registration-procedure guide (choosing an entity type,
obtaining a registered agent, name reservation, filing certificates, annual report/franchise tax
deadlines and fees). Almost none of it addresses internal governance allocation — see
`delaware_dgcl_title8.md` for the substantive corporate-governance statute this registration
process leads into. One thin point is extracted below.

---

## Q1 — Role Structure

"Delaware law requires that every business entity have and maintain a Registered Agent in the
State of Delaware... authorized to do business in the State." Every entity, regardless of its
internal governance structure, must maintain a designated, discoverable point of contact for
external communication and legal process, distinct from any of its operational or governance
roles. This is a thin but genuine structural requirement: external parties (courts, regulators,
counterparties) need one reliable, always-current contact channel independent of who currently
holds governance power internally. For a Powers constitution intended to interact with off-chain
legal/institutional counterparties, consider whether a similar "registered agent"-equivalent role
— a stable, discoverable point of contact that persists across internal role turnover — is needed,
separate from any operational or voting role.

---

## What to skip

The registration procedure itself (entity-type selection, name reservation, certificate filing,
annual report and franchise tax deadlines/fees) is Delaware administrative process with no
governance-design content.

---

## Mandate implications

- For a Powers constitution expected to interact with off-chain legal/institutional
  counterparties, consider a stable, discoverable "point of contact" role that persists across
  internal role turnover, separate from operational or voting roles.
