# Bennett, Kottasz & Yuan 2025 — Digital Innovation Capability, Organisational Agility and Performance in UK Arts and Culture

**Citation:** Bennett, R., Kottasz, R., & Yuan, P.-Y. 2025. "The Mediating Role of Digital
Innovation Capability on the Relationship Between Organisational Agility and Performance: The
Case of the UK Arts and Culture Sector." *Cogent Business & Management* 12(1): 2465893.
https://doi.org/10.1080/23311975.2025.2465893
**Analytical level:** Dynamic (very weakly actionable — see note below)
**Most relevant design decisions:** Q6

**Note on fit:** this is a quantitative organisational-behaviour/management study (10 case studies
+ a 129-organisation survey) testing whether "organisational agility" improves performance in UK
arts/culture nonprofits via the mediating effect of "digital innovation capability," in the
context of COVID-19 digital transformation. It is about *business performance management*
(revenue, audience reach, satisfaction) and *digital-transformation capability*, not about
governance structure, decision rights, or accountability design. Almost nothing in it maps to the
Q1-Q7 mandate-design framework used elsewhere in this library; the one loosely transferable point
is below.

---

## Q6 — Adaptive Capacity and Reform

p. 4 (Doz & Kosonen's three components of organisational agility, cited approvingly and confirmed
by the case studies): **sensitivity** (the capacity to detect environmental change and identify
emerging threats/opportunities), **resource fluidity** (the capacity to reallocate resources
quickly), and **leadership unity** (collective, aligned support for change enabling faster
decision-making). This is a reusable three-part checklist for whether a constitution's reform
capability is actually usable in a fast-moving crisis, distinct from whether `Adopt_Mandates`
merely *exists*: (1) does the constitution have a monitoring/proposal mandate that can surface a
changing external condition in the first place (sensitivity)? (2) can treasury or role-assignment
mandates be reconfigured quickly enough to reallocate resources once a change is detected
(resource fluidity)? (3) is the reform `votingPeriod`/quorum calibrated so that a genuinely
aligned governance body can actually act fast, rather than being structurally slow regardless of
consensus (leadership unity)? A constitution can satisfy the general "keep `Adopt_Mandates`
accessible" advice already in this library while still failing on one of these three dimensions —
this framework is useful as a supplementary diagnostic, not a replacement for the design principles
already drawn from `ostrom_2005.md`/`ostrom_2011.md`.

---

## What to skip

Nearly the entire paper: the digital-transformation and IT-innovation-adoption literature review,
the ten case-study organisational profiles, the survey methodology and structural-equation-style
mediation testing, and all findings specific to COVID-19-era digital product/service innovation
(online streaming, digital ticketing, audience-reach metrics) are business-performance research
with no governance-structure content. The finding that organisation size did not correlate with
innovation capability, and that collaborative innovation with external partners correlated with
performance, are interesting management findings but do not specify a mandate type, role
structure, or accountability mechanism — they describe an outcome correlation, not a design
recommendation actionable at the level this reading-guide library operates.

---

## Mandate implications

- When auditing whether a constitution's reform capability is crisis-usable, check it against
  three dimensions separately: can the constitution detect a change in conditions (a
  monitoring/proposal mandate), reallocate resources quickly once detected (treasury mandate
  flexibility), and reach a fast aligned decision (reform `votingPeriod` calibration) — having
  `Adopt_Mandates` present satisfies none of these three by itself.
