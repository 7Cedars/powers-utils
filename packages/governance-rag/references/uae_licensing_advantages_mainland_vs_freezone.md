# UAE Ministry of Economy & Tourism — Advantages of Licensing in Departments of Economic Development and in Free Zones

**Citation:** UAE Ministry of Economy & Tourism. "The Advantages of Licensing in Departments of
Economic Development and in Free Zones." moet.gov.ae, Regulation of Business / Companies
Registrars section.
**Analytical level:** Structural (weak fit — see note below)
**Most relevant design decisions:** Q3

**Note on fit:** this is a comparison table contrasting UAE mainland ("Department of Economic
Development") business setup against free-zone setup, across capital requirements, legal-form
menus, registration steps, and tax/ownership features. Like
`uae_free_zones_establishing_business.md`, it describes registration/tax mechanics, not internal
governance allocation — no board, general-meeting, or voting provisions appear anywhere in the
source.

---

## Q3 — Mandate Selection

The capital-requirement rows corroborate the Swiss SME Portal pattern already extracted in
`switzerland_legal_forms_overview_2023.md` (Q3: formality scales with external-trust needs) from
an entirely independent jurisdiction: a UAE public shareholding company requires a minimum capital
of 30 million dirhams, a private joint stock company 5 million, while other company types and
some free zones have no minimum at all — the same monotonic relationship between capital
formality and the type of external counterparties/trust the entity is meant to project. This adds
cross-jurisdictional corroboration to the existing principle rather than new content.

## What to skip

The tax-feature comparison (no personal income tax, no corporate tax for non-oil/non-bank
entities, 100% profit repatriation, import/export tax exemptions) and the registration-steps
checklist are UAE tax-policy and administrative-process content with no governance-design
analogue.
